DailyRepsGuy — 20 min. Workout PDF Page 1
1. Choose one movement for each body part from the phase that you are in.
(beginner, intermediate, advanced, pro)
2. Choose 1 or 2 additional movements to do so that you can focus a little
extra on a specific body part for the day.
(total 5 or 6 movements)
3. Start your timer for 20 minutes (or less but not more). My first 30 workouts
or so were all under 15 minutes. Start smaller than you think so that you
can build a habit and be sustainable. People’s biggest mistake is that they
start off too aggressive. Don’t bite off more than you can chew.
4. Get through your circuit as many times as possible. Push yourself! Rest as
little as you can. Be quick. 10 to 45 seconds between movements. Take a
sip of water and keep going.
5. Go to the next phase when movements are too easy.
6. Have fun and stay consistent. Create accountability with a friend, group,
or online! Accountability will help you succeed. Lean into accountability and
you will not regret it. Day to day you might, because it will make you do the
work when you don’t want to lol. BUT, overall you will be grateful.

HOW DO I KNOW WHEN IT’S TIME TO MOVE TO THE NEXT PHASE?
HOW TO PROGRESS BETWEEN PHASES
You don’t have to jump phases quickly. Use these micro-progressions:
Add tempo (slower lowering)
If you finish the prescribed sets and reps and still feel like you have energy
left, it’s time to progress.
Your Form Stays Solid From Start to Finish
If you can maintain clean form without wobbling, slowing down, or
compensating, you’re ready for harder variations.
You Recover Quickly
If your muscles don’t feel significantly challenged and you recover within
minutes, it’s likely too easy.
You’re No Longer Sore or Fatigued the Next Day
Not every workout should crush you, but if you consistently feel nothing the
day after, you’ve outgrown the stimulus.
Your Mind Isn’t Challenged
If the exercise feels mentally boring or automatic, you’re ready for the next
step.
Rule of Thumb:
If you can perform 2 extra reps on your final set for 2 workouts in a row, it’s
probably time to move up a phase.
Add pause reps
Add weight (kettlebell, backpack, etc.)
Add volume (more sets or reps)
Add range of motion
If you push these and still feel under-challenged, move to the next
phase.

YOU CAN BE IN DIFFERENT PHASES FOR DIFFERENT BODY PARTS
HOW TO DECIDE YOUR PHASE FOR EACH MUSCLE GROUP
Use this checklist:
Phase 1:
You’re learning the movement
You struggle to finish reps
Your form breaks early
Reasons why different phases might vary:
You have more experience with certain movements.
Some muscle groups adapt faster than others.
Your home equipment might favor one area more than another.
Injuries or mobility limitations may slow progress in one phase.
Progression is not linear and not identical across your whole body.
You might be Phase 3 for legs but Phase 1 for back — and that’s completely
normal.
What this means for your workout:
You can mix phases inside the same session depending on your strength
level per muscle group.
Example:
Legs Phase 3 (Pistol Squats, KB Swings)
Chest/Arms Phase 2 (Decline Push-Ups, Floor Press)
Abs Phase 1 (Dead Bugs, Basic Crunches)
Back Phase 2 (KB Rows)
This gives you:
The right challenge for every muscle
Faster progress
Lower injury risk
Better overall training balance

Note:
It can be a good idea to have a goal number in mind for the total amount of reps
for a workout. At first, I wouldn’t stress about this too much, just do as much as
you can.
Once you have done a few workouts and have a baseline, then you should be
striving for improvement. Once you can do 10 to 20% more reps in the time frame
than before, this can be a sign that you may want to make the workout more
challenging.
